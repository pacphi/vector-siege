# Research and Design Context

The linked GamingBolt page describes Rebellion's then-upcoming 2016 reboot: cockpit-scale combat, enemy swarms, outflanking, multiple weapons and power-ups. It is useful as a statement of modern intensity, but its pre-release scope and VR focus are not the basis for this compact browser game.

The mechanical foundation comes from documentation and museum/history coverage of the 1980 arcade game:

- A first-person tank crossed a flat plane rendered as sparse three-dimensional vectors.
- Twin-stick movement, a central cannon, radar, geometric cover, enemy tanks of different speeds, missiles, and non-hostile bonus saucers created the core loop.
- Distance was suggested by dimmer lines, while a colored overlay separated the green battlefield from warmer instrumentation.
- The hardware transformed point-and-line models with perspective math; that severe visual economy is the key aesthetic idea worth carrying into generated WebGL polygons.

Vector Siege translates those ideas into a legally distinct implementation: original name and presentation, runtime-generated geometry, original rules and tuning, synthesized sound, conventional keyboard/mouse input, and modern accessibility.
